## 1. Spec math claims vs published literature

Claim-by-claim verdicts on the design spec's threshold theory (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-25-poorman-uttt-solver-design.md, "Threshold theory" section):

1. Backup formula `T = b/(1 - a + b)` — CONFIRMED for the published model, E1.
   Avni–Henzinger "An Updated Survey of Bidding Games on Graphs" (MFCS 2022, Theorem 4) states the poorman reachability recurrence `Th(v) = Th(v+)/(1 - Th(v-) + Th(v+))`, algebraically identical with a = Th(v-), b = Th(v+).
   Existence/uniqueness proof: Avni, Henzinger, Ibsen-Jensen, "Infinite-Duration Poorman-Bidding Games" (WINE 2018, arXiv:1804.04372); ratio normalized as B1/(B1+B2), matching our p.
   Caveat: the proven model is a token game where both players choose from the same successor set, so a ≤ b holds structurally and the winner must move.
   Our partisan setting (X-children ≠ O-children, winner places own mark) is outside the proven model; the formula's extension to it is an extrapolation Stage-1 must validate.

2. Critical bid `r = (b - a)/(1 - a + b)` of combined budget — CONSISTENT, not directly published, E1 for the algebra.
   No source states this closed form.
   It is forced by claim 1: I re-derived it independently this session — at p = T, bidding r and winning yields child ratio (T - r)/(1 - r) = a exactly; losing to any bid y ≥ r yields p/(1 - y) ≥ b.
   The Richman analog (optimal bid = (R(v) - R(u)) × total money, Lazarus–Loeb–Propp–Ullman "Richman Games" 1996, arXiv:math/9502222) is the pattern cite.
   Treat as a lemma the findings doc proves, not a citation.

3. Zugzwang branch (a > b ⇒ both bid 0, tie owner forced to move, T = a if h = X else b) — NOT-COVERED by any source; not contradicted.
   In the Avni et al. model zugzwang cannot arise (shared successor set, mandatory move).
   The literature resolves reluctant-mover situations by three other mechanisms, each value-changing: Develin–Payne 2010 (bid winner may delegate the move to the opponent), Larsson–Wästlund "Endgames in bidding chess" 2019 (negative bids), Kant–Larsson–Rai–Upasany EJC 2024 (spendable tie-breaking marker announced inside bids).
   Our alternation tie-owner rule is a novel mechanism with no published counterpart; the claimed T = a / T = b values are plausible limits but must be proven by our own induction plus Stage-1 exhaustive check.

4. Budget-independence of T given one tie-owner bit — CONFIRMED without the bit, E1; the h argument itself is novel.
   Continuous thresholds are pure ratio functions by construction (survey Definitions 2–3), and WINE 2018 states results "are not affected by the tie-breaking mechanism that is used" — away from the exact boundary.
   No published threshold theorem carries a tie-owner state variable; T(s, h) is our extension.
   Contrast to flag: Develin–Payne's discrete tie-breaking advantage is a persistent, strategically spendable token, not parity-driven alternation — cross-checking against their model would be a category error.

5. Terminal `T = 1/2` chip-comparison rule (chip leader wins draws, exact tie ½–½) — NOT-COVERED, E1 for the absence.
   Explicit search over budget-in-payoff variants found nothing: "Bidding Games with Charging" (CONCUR 2024, arXiv:2407.06288) changes budgets during play but the objective is still the path objective; Meir–Kalai–Tennenholtz (GEB 2018), scoring games (Rai–Larsson–Patel 2021), and all-pay variants likewise give leftover budget no terminal value.
   Lazarus et al. 1996 is explicitly the opposite: "At the game's end, money loses all value."
   Warning precedent: the charging paper shows budget-coupled variants can break threshold fixed-point uniqueness.
   The whole T-theory's survival under this terminal is ours to prove; the third outcome (draw at exactly equal chips) is outside two-outcome threshold theorems.

Additional literature facts that shape the design:

- Exact boundary p = T is deliberately undefined in the literature — outcome "depends on how we resolve draws in biddings" (WINE 2018) and on coin tosses in the Richman original.
  On DAGs (our game is finite and acyclic in (cells, budgets)), Lazarus et al. 1996 note strict-inequality winners win "definitely", so only the exact-equality set is tie-rule-sensitive.
- Discrete chips converge to the continuous threshold only in the large-chip limit (Develin–Payne Thm 3.10), with genuine small-stack anomalies: continuous R(TTT) = 133/256, yet the optimal first move flips with total chip count (their Thms 6.1/6.7).
  At 10^9 units we are deep in the asymptotic regime, but the discrete brute-force cross-check must not assume the continuous value near the boundary.
- Poorman has NO random-turn-game equivalence (unlike Richman; poorman ratios are typically irrational, survey remark) — closes off any Monte-Carlo random-turn shortcut for the engine pair.
- Richman/Poorman foundations: Lazarus, Loeb, Propp, Ullman 1996 (arXiv:math/9502222, Richman, pay-opponent, coin-flip ties); Lazarus, Loeb, Propp, Stromquist, Ullman, "Combinatorial games under auction play" (GEB 1999, paywalled — poorman variant developed there per the 1996 paper's own announcement and both Avni–Henzinger surveys).
- Simultaneous-move algorithm cites for the findings doc: Zinkevich et al. NeurIPS 2007 (CFR), Hart–Mas-Colell Econometrica 2000 (regret matching), Lisý et al. NeurIPS 2013 and Lanctot et al. 2013 (SM-MCTS).
- Alternating-move UTTT: Bertholon et al. 2020 (arXiv:2006.02353) prove a first-player win in ≤ 43 moves — but ONLY for the ruleset where a won local board stays playable while it has empty cells.
  Our spec rule 6 (board closed when won or full) is the other popular variant, which remains unsolved; the findings doc must not cite Bertholon as covering our rules.

## 2. Rules edge cases the fixtures must cover

Enumerated from spec canonical rules 1–9 (E1, spec lines 19–35):

1. First move forced into the center local board, with first-move bid tie resolved by coin flip (rules 4b, 5); fixtures must carry both coin-flip branches.
2. Tie ownership as pure alternation: after any auction, the mover loses tie ownership; consecutive 0–0 tie chains alternate the mover.
3. Both budgets exactly 0 (rule 9): degenerates to alternating UTTT; fixture a full playout segment.
4. One budget 0, opponent positive: zero-budget player can only bid 0; opponent chooses between winning with 1 unit or contesting the tie at 0.
5. Zugzwang position with a > b: both bid 0, tie owner forced to move; fixtures for both h = X and h = O.
6. Send-to-closed-board free choice: mark in cell c where local board c is closed (won, or full without winner — both closure kinds) ⇒ any empty cell in any open board.
7. Self-closing send: a move in local board b at cell index b that closes board b itself, immediately forcing free choice.
8. Full-without-winner local board: closed, contributes no macro mark to either player.
9. Macro three-in-a-row ends the game outright regardless of budgets (rule 7), including double-line completions from one local win.
10. All nine boards closed, no macro winner: chip comparison — leader wins 1–0; exactly equal chips ½–½ (rule 8); fixtures at margin +1 unit, −1 unit, and 0.
11. Knife-edge threshold cases: small discrete-budget positions where p sits exactly at the computed critical share, pinning the engine/referee behavior at equality.
12. Bid legality: integer units, 0 ≤ bid ≤ current budget; all-in bids; winner pays own bid to bank (money destroyed — verify accounting, not loser-pays).
13. Move-on-win rule: auction winner must place a legal mark in the forced board (or any open board when unconstrained); intended-move legality is checkable at submission since state is common knowledge.
14. Forced-board index propagation after every move, including into the board the mark itself just closed (item 7) and into boards closed earlier.
15. Terminal-detection ordering: macro win checked before board-full/draw accounting within the same move.

External prior art (research-agent sweep with URLs, spot-verified):

- No existing exact Poorman auction-TTT threshold solver exists anywhere, under any tie rule.
- Closest artifact: github.com/dm0n3y/biddingTTT (Python, exact 3×3 bidding-TTT values, real-valued and Develin–Payne discrete modes) — Richman (pay-opponent) with star-chip ties, so a cross-check oracle only if we add a Richman mode; no license stated, so reference-only, no code reuse.
- Second Richman cross-check: mindsortlabs/BiddingTicTacToe (Java DP over all 3^9 boards, Richman, buried in an Android app).
- Avni et al., "Reachability Poorman Discrete-Bidding Games" (ECAI 2023, arXiv:2307.15218) is the first paper on exactly our mechanism class (discrete poorman, pay-bank, threshold budgets) but with ties fixed in favor of Player 1; the paper mentions an implementation, no public artifact found — worth an author email, not a dependency.
- Bidding chess/hex (Bhat–Payne, Payne–Robeva): expository papers, no public engines; the Richman random-turn trick they use does not transfer to poorman.
- OpenSpiel has no Richman/Poorman/auction-per-move game (closest: oshi-zumo, goofspiel); Gambit cannot enumerate the (board × budgets) tree; both would still require us to implement the game.
- Alternating-move UTTT rules-core references for the engine pair: nelhage/ultimattt (Rust, Apache-2.0, minimax + DFPN provers) is the strongest architecture reference; ultimate-ttt/ultimate-ttt already in spec.
- Every discrete-bidding artifact found uses the Develin–Payne star chip or ties-favor-P1 — none uses our alternation tie rule or the leftover-chips draw-breaker.
