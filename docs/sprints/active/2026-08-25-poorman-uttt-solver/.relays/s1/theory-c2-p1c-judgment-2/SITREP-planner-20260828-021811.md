## Addendum to the open judgment item

The adversarial panel's math lens (which independently reproduced the sweep's N=1/N=4 rows exactly and validated the predicate, buckets, and state classes end-to-end) surfaced structure that judgment-1's aggregate framing hides. It comes from the lens's own computation over the solver outputs — the committed artifact cannot express it, because T-value is not a dimension of the locked group partition:

- The exact-equality population is ~59% T=1/2 knife points at every scale. Those disagree with the tie-owner-wins convention at only ~2.3-2.8%, slowly DECLINING with scale (2.84% at N=2 down to 2.32% at N=32).
- The remaining T != 1/2 knife points disagree at ~47% (46.9% over N=16-32) — statistically near coin-flip. The convention has essentially no predictive power there.
- The headline "stable ~20.4%" is therefore stability of the mixture weights, not of one error mechanism. Two secondary notes: exact X/O mark-swap symmetry pairs every bucket (distinct positions up to symmetry are half the nominal counts), and the odd/even oscillation in the exhaustive rows is a parity effect on the T=1/2 population share.

## Why this matters for the ruling

- It strengthens judgment-1's option 2 (bounded follow-up analysis): the disagreement structure is not noise — it cleanly separates into a benign, shrinking class and a coin-flip class, and a class-aware convention (or a documented "convention is only trustworthy at T=1/2 ties" caveat) is a cheap, well-posed follow-up.
- It weakens a plain option 1 (keep as-is with the aggregate caveat): "~1-in-5 error" understates the risk exactly where the convention is most load-bearing (asymmetric thresholds), and overstates it at symmetric ties.
- Option 3 (re-open the tie rule now) remains heavier than the evidence demands; the ratified convention's labeled status is not contradicted.

This seat's recommendation is unchanged but sharpened: option 2, with the follow-up scoped to a class-level (T-value-aware) breakdown and a recommendation on whether the large-scale convention caveat should be class-qualified.
